4. STATEMENT OF WORK ACCOMPLISHED / FINAL PROGRESS BILLING

This is one of the most important documents.

The basic principle is:

Do not simply put 100% beside every BOQ item.

Each item must be supported by actual accomplishment/measurement.

The current RA 12009 IRR says the SWA should cover the cumulative value of the works executed based on the BOQ and approved variations.

Recommended SWA structure
Item	Description	Unit	Contract Qty	Unit Price	Previous Qty	Current Qty	Cumulative Qty	% Accomplished	Amount
A.1	Mobilization	LS	1	₱xxx	1	0	1	100%	₱xxx
A.2	Excavation	m³	500	₱xxx	450	50	500	100%	₱xxx
A.3	Concrete	m³	300	₱xxx	250	50	300	100%	₱xxx
A.4	Reinforcement Steel	kg	20,000	₱xxx	18,000	2,000	20,000	100%	₱xxx
Formula

Current accomplishment:

Cumulative quantity – Previous certified quantity

Amount:

Cumulative quantity × Contract Unit Price

But for a final billing you must also account for:

Gross value of completed work
+ Approved Variation Orders
+ Approved price adjustments, if applicable
------------------------------------------------
Gross final accomplishment

LESS:
Previous payments
Advance payment recoupment
Retention
Third-party liabilities
Uncorrected defects
Taxes/other authorized deductions
------------------------------------------------
NET FINAL PAYMENT

COA guidance specifically emphasizes that payment must be based on actual quantities/work accomplished, and that geotagging can be used to validate the physical existence/location of works.

5. STATEMENT OF TIME ELAPSED

This is not the same as the percentage accomplishment.

It establishes the relationship between:

contract time vs. actual elapsed time vs. physical accomplishment.

Template
Particular	Date / No. of Days
Notice to Proceed	[DATE]
Original Contract Duration	[___] Calendar Days
Original Start Date	[DATE]
Original Completion Date	[DATE]
Approved Time Extension No. 1	[___] Days
Approved Time Extension No. 2	[___] Days
Revised Completion Date	[DATE]
Actual Completion Date	[DATE]
Total Contract Time	[___] Days
Actual Time Elapsed	[___] Days
Time Remaining	0 Days
Physical Accomplishment	100%
Time Elapsed	[___]%
Slippage	[___]%
Liquidated Damages	₱0.00 / ₱[amount]
Formula

If there were no extensions:

Time elapsed %

Actual elapsed calendar days ÷ Contract duration × 100

Example:

Contract duration = 120 days
Elapsed = 120 days

120 ÷ 120 × 100
= 100%

But if there were approved suspensions/extensions, do not calculate against the original completion date.

Use the approved revised completion date.

18. CERTIFICATE OF ACCEPTANCE

This is even more important.

Completion and acceptance are different.

Completion

Means:

Contractor has completed the contractual work.

Acceptance

Means:

Procuring Entity has inspected and accepted the completed work.

This distinction matters because under the current RA 12009 framework, retention is tied to final acceptance.

19. RETENTION — THIS IS WHERE MANY FINAL BILLINGS GO WRONG

Under Section 71.2.8 of the current RA 12009 IRR:

progress payments are subject to 10% retention;
retention continues until 50% of work is completed;
after 50%, if work is satisfactory and on schedule, no additional retention is made;
otherwise the 10% retention continues, subject to the rule allowing reduction to 5% based on justifiable causes;
total retention becomes due for release upon final acceptance.

Therefore:

Scenario A — Final billing, but no final acceptance

You cannot simply write:

"Please release 100% including retention."

That can be problematic.

Scenario B — Final billing + final acceptance

Then the retention becomes due for release, subject to the applicable contract and accounting/audit requirements.

Scenario C — Retention substituted

The contractor may request substitution of retention by:

irrevocable standby Letter of Credit;
bank guarantee; or
surety bond callable on demand,

of equivalent amount, subject to the requirements of the IRR and government acceptance.

20. DO NOT CONFUSE DEFECTS LIABILITY WITH RETENTION

This is another major issue.

The current GPPB infrastructure PBD provides a one-year defects liability period from project completion up to final acceptance.

During that period, the contractor is responsible for correcting qualifying defects at its own expense.

Therefore:

CONSTRUCTION
     ↓
COMPLETION
     ↓
DEFECTS LIABILITY PERIOD
     ↓
FINAL ACCEPTANCE
     ↓
RETENTION RELEASE
     ↓
POST-FINAL-ACCEPTANCE WARRANTY

The exact warranty/security treatment must be checked against the actual contract, SCC and applicable RA 12009 IRR provisions.
